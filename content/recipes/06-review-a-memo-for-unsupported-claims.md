## What you are trying to accomplish

A memo that survives a skeptical reader: every claim that matters has a source or is marked as an inference, every ambiguity is resolved or named, and every decision the memo asks for is stated as a decision with an owner. The assistant reads as the toughest person in the meeting would, before that person does.

## What the assistant needs from you

- The memo, and the sources it rests on, within the data boundary.
- The audience: who will read it and what they are being asked to do.
- What kind of review you want: a light pass for clarity, or the full skeptical read.

## What the assistant can carry

- Reading every claim and asking "what is this based on?" Listing the ones with no answer as {{label kind=open}}.
- Flagging ambiguity: sentences with two readings, undefined terms, numbers without units or dates, "recently" and "significant" and "most."
- Finding the missing decisions: places where the memo implies a choice has been made or must be made, without saying who makes it.
- Proposing specific rewrites, each marked as a proposal for you to accept or not.

## What you decide or approve

- Which findings to act on. Some ambiguity is deliberate and appropriate; you know which.
- Whether an unsupported claim gets a source, gets softened to an inference, or comes out.
- The final text. The assistant may draft or propose changes. You review and approve the final text, remain accountable for its use, and preserve contribution provenance where material.

## What to check before you use it

!!! verify "Check before it leaves your hands"
    - For each claim the assistant marked Open, decide: source it, label it, or cut it. No fourth option.
    - Read the memo's ask aloud. Is it clear who is being asked to decide what, by when?
    - Confirm that no real personal, customer, or payroll detail is in the memo that should not be.

## What to keep in the record

!!! record "The record for this recipe"
    The review note and the version of the memo that went out. If a claim was cut for lack of a source, note it; the question will come back.

## When to stop and ask

!!! stop "Stop and ask when"
    - The memo asks a reader to approve something consequential on the strength of a claim nobody can source. Do not send it. Get the source or change the ask.
    - The review reveals that the decision the memo asks for has already been made elsewhere. Find out where before circulating.

## Authority in this recipe

| Level | In this recipe |
| --- | --- |
| The assistant may, without asking | read; list unsourced claims and ambiguities; propose rewrites marked as proposals |
| You review | every finding; every rewrite; the final text |
| Needs an authorized decision-maker | whatever the memo itself asks for |
| Stop and escalate | a consequential ask resting on an unsourced claim |

!!! example "What it looks like"
    *Assistant:* "Nine findings. Three claims have no source in the folder: the 40 percent figure in paragraph two, 'most teams' in paragraph four, and the vendor's renewal date. Two ambiguities: 'recently' in the opening, and 'the system,' which refers to two different systems in this memo. One missing decision: paragraph six says the process 'will change in Q1' without saying who decided or who approves. Rewrites proposed for all nine, marked as proposals."
