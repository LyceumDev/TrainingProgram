## Is this going to replace my job?

This portal cannot promise how roles or staffing will change. This program is designed to help employees use approved AI systems while keeping human purpose, judgment, authority, and accountability visible. What it does change is which parts of the work an assistant carries (the first draft, the summary, the cross-check, the reorganizing) and which parts stay visibly yours: knowing what the work is for, what the constraints really are, what the stakeholder meant, and what is acceptable here. Questions about workforce decisions belong with organizational leadership and Human Resources.

## Do I need to be technical?

No. You need to be able to say what you want, why, and what good looks like, and to read a result critically. The environment your team uses is set up for you; the setup page for it will be written so that no one has to recognize technical terms first. A person who does not write code can direct technically complex work.

## Which AI product do we use?

The honest answer today: it depends on the work, and for business users the environment is still being assessed. The [environments page](~/environments/index.html) says what is approved, what is in use, and what is waiting. Until your environment is approved, the guided first session uses the guide's, on your real work, with non-sensitive material.

## Can I paste customer or employee information?

No, unless the environment has been explicitly approved for exactly that data. Describe the kind of thing, use placeholders, and point with reference numbers. [Lesson 5](~/lessons/05-safety-and-organizational-data.html) has the whole rule; it is short.

## What if the assistant is wrong?

It will be, sometimes, and confidently. Ask "what is that based on?" A good answer names a source; a weak one restates the claim. Verify what matters in proportion to what happens if it is wrong, correct it plainly, and, if it matters, note the correction in the record. [Lesson 3](~/lessons/03-confident-errors-and-verification.html) makes this an ordinary habit.

## What if it does something I did not ask?

In an approved environment the assistant works inside boundaries you set: read, draft, recommend, and only narrowly act. If it acts outside them, stop, and use the [urgent route](~/support/index.html#urgent). That is not an overreaction; it is how the boundaries get corrected for everyone.

## Does it remember our work?

Two different things. The product may have its own memory, which is convenient and belongs to the vendor. Your **project record**, a set of files your project owns, supplies the context used to resume the work, whether the environment loads it automatically or you provide it, and it survives a change of product. [Lesson 2](~/lessons/02-context-continuity-and-the-record.html) explains the difference and how to keep one.

## Do people name their AI assistants?

Some working teams in this program use a name for the assistant and some do not; some decided later. All three are complete answers. Where a name is used, it was arrived at with the assistant rather than assigned to it, and whatever was decided is what everyone uses. *Assistant* on this portal is the general term; the working model is collaborative either way, as the [About page](~/about.html#words) explains.

## How is my use measured?

Lightly and transparently, and never for performance evaluation. The program looks at whether real work is happening with an assistant over time, whether claims carry sources, whether boundaries hold, and whether people feel in control of the work. Aggregate learning, not individual scorekeeping. The [leaders page](~/leaders/index.html) states the same rule to leaders.

## Who do I ask?

Your program guide, any time. [Support](~/support/index.html) has every route, including the urgent one.
