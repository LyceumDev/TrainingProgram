## You have the model. Now use it on something real.

The foundation is not the point; the work is. Here is the whole path from this page to a finished piece of work with an assistant.

## 1. Choose the case that looks like your week

[Modernization work](~/cases/modernization.html), [business analysis and specification writing](~/cases/business-analysis.html), or [customer support](~/cases/customer-support.html). If more than one applies, start with the one whose next task is already on your desk.

## 2. Find one task guide

Every case page lists the first three task guides to try, and the [task guide library](~/guides/index.html) has all of them. A task guide tells you what to do first, what the assistant carries, what to check, what to keep, and when to stop. Pick the one closest to the task you wrote down in lesson 1.

## 3. Know where the assistant will work, or say you don't

If your team already has an approved environment, the [environments page](~/environments/index.html) links its setup guide. If you are not sure, **"I don't know yet"** is a complete answer: [say it here](~/support/i-dont-know-yet.html) and a person helps you choose.

## 4. Book a guided first session

This is the recommended start for everyone. You bring one real, permitted task. A program guide sits with you and the assistant for about an hour. The session aims to leave you with:

- a useful piece of work, verified, that you can actually use;
- a project record you can resume from tomorrow;
- a task guide, or an improvement to one, drawn from your task;
- a clear next step for you and your work with the assistant.

[Request a guided first session.](~/support/request-a-guided-session.html)

## What good looks like after thirty days

Not a certificate. Real work with an AI assistant in most weeks. Artifacts whose claims carry sources. A record that lets you pick up where you left off. A sense that you are in charge of the work and could explain what the assistant did and what you decided. If that is not how it feels, tell your guide. Improving the experience is the program's job, not yours.
