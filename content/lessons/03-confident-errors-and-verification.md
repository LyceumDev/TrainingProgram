## Smooth is not the same as true

An AI assistant writes fluently. That is useful, and it is also the trap: a wrong claim can come out in the same confident sentence as a right one. Fluent language is not evidence. So the professional response is not suspicion of everything, and not trust in everything. It is a habit: **verify the things that matter, in proportion to what happens if they are wrong.**

A typo in a draft heading costs nothing. A wrong effective date in a memo that leaders act on costs a great deal. Spend your checking where the consequence is.

## Ask for the source

The single most useful sentence you will say to an assistant is: **"What is that based on?"**

A good answer names a document, a page, a table, or a check that was run. A weak answer restates the claim more confidently. When the assistant cannot point to a source, the claim is not verified, whatever it sounds like. Say so plainly, and either find the source together or mark the item open.

## The four labels

Everything in this program, lessons, task guides, records, and the seminar, uses one shared vocabulary for the state of a piece of work. Learn these four and you can read any artifact a human–AI working team produces.

{{labels}}

Two of these are about evidence and two are about decisions, and they are named so that one is never mistaken for the other. A decision is authoritative because someone with authority made it, not because every premise behind it is proven. An inference is reasoning you can follow, not a guess.

!!! verify "What checking looks like"
    Open the source the assistant named and read the line it relied on. Run the calculation once yourself. Compare the summary against the original paragraph. Ask the colleague who owns the topic. A check is usually quick. A wrong claim in circulation is not.

## The ordinary correction

When the assistant is wrong, say so plainly: "That date is wrong, the memo says the third." No drama, no lecture. The assistant corrects it, and, if it matters, the correction goes in the record so the same mistake is not made twice. Trustworthy collaboration is not the absence of errors. It is errors that get caught, said out loud, and fixed.

!!! do "Something to do today"
    Take one paragraph an AI assistant produced for you recently and label each sentence: Verified, Inferred, Decided, or Open. Notice how many were Open. That is the normal amount.
