Requirements before products

The environment-readiness standard

What an environment must satisfy, or explicitly disposition, before it is presented here as an approved place for an AI assistant to work.

Before any product is presented on this portal as an approved environment for an AI assistant, it must satisfy, or explicitly disposition, each of these ten requirements. The standard is written first, and the products are chosen against it, so that the program never depends on a single vendor or interface.

  1. Organizational approval. IT and Security approve the environment for the intended users and data.
  2. Persistent working context. Related instructions, sources, and work can be carried across sessions in a controlled way.
  3. Authorized source access. The assistant can reach only sources the person is permitted to use.
  4. Role-based permissions. Access follows the person’s organizational identity and least-privilege needs.
  5. Action boundaries. Read, draft, recommend, and act are distinguishable; consequential actions receive appropriate review or approval.
  6. Traceability. Sources, material decisions, and important actions can be inspected later.
  7. Data and retention controls. The environment’s handling, retention, and administrative settings match organizational policy.
  8. Accessible use. Employees with disabilities can perform the complete workflow.
  9. Support and revocation. Ownership, support, incident response, and removal procedures are known.
  10. Portable continuity. Decisions, sources, open items, and the current baseline live in a durable record owned by the person’s project, not solely in opaque vendor memory.

Why the tenth requirement is there

A product’s memory may be useful, but it is not the authoritative project record. A product change must not erase a working team’s ability to resume the work. The program teaches the project record from lesson 2 so that no one learns this the hard way.

Who assesses

IT leads the environment assessment with Security’s input. The program supplies the work requirements from the cases and task guides. The result may be more than one approved environment, because business documents, repositories, and operational systems do not have identical needs. Status is published on the environments page as each assessment concludes.
