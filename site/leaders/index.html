For leaders

What a human–AI working team is, what it can produce, what you must provide or decide, and what evidence would justify scaling.

What a working team is

A human–AI working team is one of your people and an AI assistant working together over time on real tasks, with the person holding purpose, judgment, authority, and accountability, and the assistant carrying research, drafting, analysis, structure, and, within approved boundaries, actions. It is not a tool rollout and not an automation project. The unit of value is the working team, and its workflow improves with practice and a durable record.

What it can produce

In business analysis: problem statements, traced requirements, sourced comparisons, specifications with traceability, and reviewed memos, with every claim labeled as verified, inferred, decided, or open. In the program’s field experience so far, first drafts and traced lists have taken hours rather than days; results depend on the task and the environment. In modernization and support, the same shape of gain once those environments are approved. What it does not produce is decisions. Those remain with the people who hold them.

What you must provide or decide

  • Time. A guided first session (about an hour) and permission to use an assistant on real work in the weeks after. The evidence behind this program says sustained use, not course completion, is what produces results.
  • Tasks. Real, permitted work your people already do. The best first tasks took time but not judgment.
  • Boundaries. What the working team may read, draft, recommend, and act on in your area, and which decisions stay with you. The recipes carry defaults; you confirm them for your context.
  • A door. Your explicit word that asking for help, reporting a near miss, or saying “I don’t know which environment I need” is expected.

What authority is never implied

An assistant’s ability to take an action is never permission to take it. Nothing on this portal authorizes a tool, a data use, or an action. Capability is not authority, and the person in the working team remains accountable for what enters the organization’s work.

What safe adoption requires

Approved environments assessed against a written readiness standard before anyone is told a product is approved. The one absolute on personal, customer, employee, and payroll information. A project record owned by the project, not the vendor. Security’s review of the standards before publication. An urgent route that people use because good-faith reporting is expected and handled by policy.

How results are measured, and how they are not

Measurement is light, transparent, and never used for individual performance evaluation. The program looks at whether real work continues with an assistant over the first thirty days, how quickly a first useful verified artifact appears, whether claims carry sources, whether boundaries hold, and whether people report a sense of control and a willingness to challenge the assistant. It reports aggregate program learning. It does not produce individual scorecards, and it must not be used to build them.

What evidence would justify scaling

A founding cohort whose members are still doing real work with an assistant after thirty days; artifacts that reviewers judge useful and properly sourced; boundaries that held, with near misses reported and handled without blame; recipes contributed by people outside the build team; and a repeatable onboarding and guided-session playbook. When that evidence exists, a one-page scaling case follows. Until then, the program stays small enough to learn from.
