Lesson 4 of 6

Agentic work and authority

What tools and actions are, why being able to act is not permission to act, and how boundaries and approvals work.

When an assistant can do, not only say

Some AI assistants can only talk. Others can use tools and act: search a system, read a file, fill a form, run a check, open a ticket, send a message. An assistant working this way is often called an AI agent, and the work is called agentic. It is where most of the time savings live, and where most of the care belongs.

The model is the same one from lesson 1. What changes is that the output is no longer only words on a screen. It can be a change in a system that other people rely on.

Capability is not authority

An agent that can send an email has not been authorized to send it. Authority is something you establish, explicitly, before the work starts. The useful way to think about it is four levels:

Level What it means Example
Read The assistant may look, search, and summarize. “Read the three policy documents and tell me where they disagree.”
Draft The assistant may produce work for you to review. “Draft the reply. I will read it before anything is sent.”
Recommend The assistant may propose an action and its reasons. “Tell me which of these tickets should be escalated, and why.”
Act The assistant may carry out an approved action, within a boundary. “File the approved summary in the project folder.”

Most work lives in the first three. Acting is granted narrowly, for specific things, in environments your organization has approved for it.

Consequential actions get a person

Anything hard to undo, visible to others, or touching money, people, or records is a consequential action. Those get reviewed by you before they happen, and some of them need a decision from someone with the authority to make it. The assistant’s job at that moment is to stop and show you what it is about to do. Yours is to look.

The assistant should stop and ask when

  • it is about to do something it was not explicitly permitted to do;
  • the action would be hard to reverse;
  • it is unsure which of two instructions applies;
  • it needs information it should not have (lesson 5);
  • the result would go to someone outside the working team.

Every task guide states its boundaries

Each task guide in this portal ends with the boundaries to confirm before the assistant begins: what it may do once you have approved them, what you must review, what needs an authorized decision-maker, and what must stop and escalate. Read them before you start, and there are far fewer surprises in what the assistant does.

Something to do today

Pick a task you might hand to an assistant and write one line for each level: what it may read, what it may draft, what it may recommend, and whether there is anything at all it may do on its own. Most people find the last line is short. That is correct.
