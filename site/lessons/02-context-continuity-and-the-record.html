Lesson 2 of 6

Context, continuity, and the durable record

Why context changes results, the difference between a product's memory and your project's record, and how work resumes tomorrow.

Context is most of the result

Two people ask an assistant to “draft the policy update.” One gets a generic page. The other gets a usable draft in the house style, because they said what the policy is for, who reads it, what changed since last year, and which earlier document to follow. The assistant did not get smarter between those two requests. It got context.

The practical rule: tell the assistant why, not just what. The purpose, the audience, the constraints, and the source documents. Ten seconds of context often prevents a good deal of rework.

Two kinds of memory, and only one is yours

Most AI products now offer some form of memory: the product remembers things about you and your past conversations. It is convenient. It is also opaque, owned by the vendor, tied to that product, and not something you can hand to a colleague or take with you when the product changes.

The project record is different. It is a set of files that belong to your project, in a place your organization approves, that you and the assistant use at the start of work and update at the end. It holds:

  • Decisions, with who made them and why.
  • Sources: the documents and references the work stands on.
  • Open items: what is unresolved, and who owns the next step.
  • The current baseline: the version of the work that is real right now.
  • Material corrections: what turned out to be wrong, and what replaced it.

The record belongs to your project, not the product

A product’s memory can help, but it is never the authoritative record. If your organization changes products next year, the project record is what lets your work, and your ability to pick it up with an assistant again, survive the move. Keep it where your project keeps its other files.

How work resumes

A working session has a shape. At the start, the record is opened: here is where we were, here is what is open. Depending on the approved environment, the assistant may load that context itself or you may provide it. At the end, the record is updated: here is what we decided, here is what changed. It takes a minute or two, and it is the difference between an assistant that is briefed tomorrow and one that starts from nothing.

Where the environment supports it, the assistant may offer to update the record at natural stopping points. Say yes. And when it seems to have forgotten something, that is usually a gap in the record or in the context you gave it, not carelessness. Supply the context, then fix the record so it does not happen again.

Something to do today

Make a folder for one project you will bring to an assistant, in the place your team keeps project files. Put three things in it: the purpose in two sentences, the documents that matter, and a short list of what is open. That is a record. It is already useful.
