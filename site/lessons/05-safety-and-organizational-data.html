Lesson 5 of 6

Safety and organizational data

Approved environments, what information is allowed, credentials, sensitive data, records, and how to escalate.

The calm version of the rules

Nothing in this lesson is meant to make you nervous. It is meant to make you certain. If you know these five things, you can work with an assistant all day without a second thought.

1. Work in an approved environment

Your assistant works in a place your organization has approved for it. You sign in through the organization’s approved method. Never share credentials in the conversation. The assistant receives only the access explicitly provided through an approved connection or environment.

The environments page lists what is approved and what is still being assessed. If your environment is not on it, the answer is not “use it quietly.” The answer is to ask.

2. Some information never goes into the conversation

The one absolute

Real customer, employee, and payroll information does not go into an AI conversation unless the environment has been explicitly approved for exactly that data. No names tied to pay data. No Social Security numbers. No account numbers. No real amounts for real people.

Describe the kind of thing. Use placeholders (“Employee A”). Point with reference numbers: the ticket, the case ID, the record number. You and the organization’s approved controls hold this boundary; do not rely on the assistant to catch a slip. If a slip happens, report it and fix it immediately. No exceptions, no busy-day discounts.

3. Credentials and secrets stay out of the conversation

Never paste passwords, keys, tokens, access codes, or connection strings into the conversation or the project record. If access is required, use the approved sign-in, delegated connection, or secrets mechanism. If none exists, stop and ask.

4. The record is an organizational record

What you and the assistant produce is work product of the organization, with the same recordkeeping rules as anything else you write. Keep the project record where your project keeps its files. Keep decisions traceable to the people who made them. Credit human and AI contributions to their actual authors when the work warrants authorship.

5. Know the door

Something feels off. An assistant suggests an action you are not sure is allowed. You realize sensitive information went somewhere it should not have. You are not sure whether a document is approved for this use.

Urgent route

Stop the work. Report promptly through the urgent route: tell your program guide by the fastest route you have, and the guide involves Security. Good-faith early reporting is expected and helps limit harm; handling follows organizational policy.

Something to do today

Look at the task you chose in lesson 1. Does it involve real personal, customer, or payroll data? If yes, decide now how you will describe it with placeholders and reference numbers. If no, you are ready.
