Lesson 1 of 6

Meet the working model

What an AI assistant is, what you bring, what it can carry, and what stays yours.

A collaborator, not a vending machine

You have probably used an AI chat box: type a question, get an answer, close the tab. An AI assistant, used the way this program teaches, is the same kind of system used differently. It works alongside you over days and weeks on a project, works from the context you give it, and takes on the parts of the work that are heavy but not yours to decide.

A useful way to picture it: a capable new colleague who joined last week. Sharp, fast, tireless, and unfamiliar with how things are actually done here until you tell them.

What you bring

  • The purpose. What this work is for, and who it is for.
  • The context. What happened before, what constraints apply, which document is the real one.
  • The judgment. What good looks like, what is acceptable, what is out of the question.
  • The authority. What the assistant may do on its own, what you will review, and what needs someone else’s decision.

None of this gets smaller when an assistant is in the room. It gets more valuable, because it is now the part of the work that only you can do.

What the assistant can carry

  • Research across sources you point it to, with summaries you can check.
  • First drafts, second drafts, and the reorganizing in between.
  • Analysis: comparing options, finding gaps, spotting inconsistencies.
  • Structure: turning a rough request into a problem statement, a list of requirements, or a decision record.
  • When connected to approved tools, narrowly authorized actions, such as filing a document or opening a ticket.

What stays accountable human work

Deciding. Approving anything with consequences. Judging whether the result is actually good. Being the name on the work that enters the organization. The rule that holds the whole model together:

You establish the assistant’s authority, review consequential actions, and remain accountable for what enters the organization’s work.

Capability is not authority

An assistant may be able to send the email, change the record, or run the process. Being able to is not permission to. You set the boundary, and the assistant works inside it. Lesson 4 shows how that works in practice.

A first honest expectation

Your assistant will sometimes be confidently wrong. That is a known property of these systems, not a reason to distrust everything it says. Lesson 3 gives you the habit that makes this ordinary: ask for the source, check what matters, correct it, move on.

Something to do today

Write down one task from this week that took time but not judgment: a summary, a comparison, a first draft, a tidy-up. That is the task to bring to your first session.
